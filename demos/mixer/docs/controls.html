The 20-second Retina demonstration shows two viewers, four downstream keys, a manual fade, halfway hold and return stroke. Its native pictures were captured before Janus; controls and measured performance meters are replayed in the actual UI. The controls below describe the live operator page.

Preview, Program and automatic takes

A scene tile or 1–9 selects Preview. Direct / Shift+D makes a scene pick cut straight to Program; the arrow keys still browse Preview without taking it. Scene filters and page buttons let you browse the catalogue. After a completed background take, Preview becomes the scene that left Program.

Choose Fade / F, Dip / D, or a media wipe (W, repeated to cycle the list), then press Auto / Space to run it. Selecting a transition prepares Auto; it does not take. Fade mixes the scenes. Dip goes through black, white or a chosen colour. The curve buttons shape a fade or each half of a dip. A wipe uses its clip's duration and cut point; clip paths refer to files on the mixer host.

Cut / C takes immediately, or completes a transition already on air. The Halt / Complete selector controls a second Auto press during its own transition: Halt pauses it with both sources still moving, and another press resumes; Complete finishes it immediately.

The rate field uses the show's frame rate: 15f means 15 frames, 1:15 means one second and 15 frames. Bare 45 is 45 frames, displayed as 1:15 at 30 fps. Presets, the small arrows and the mouse wheel change the rate; Shift with the wheel steps one second.

Manual T-bar

Use the rotate button to place the T-bar horizontally or upright. Drag from the end where it rests to mix toward Preview. Stop anywhere to hold the mixture while both sources keep moving. Reach the far end to complete the take; the next take starts from that end in the opposite direction. Return to the starting end to cancel the take. The grip, percentage, LEDs and trail show its position and progress.

Auto can run a manual fade on from its current position; Cut completes it. If an automatic take finishes while the physical lever is between endpoints, the lever must return to an endpoint before starting another take. The page parks its on-screen lever as needed.

A selected wipe can follow the lever forward and backward once its complete clip is decoded in the GPU wipe cache. The lever is unavailable while that wipe is not ready. Without a selected wipe, manual operation mixes the scenes. The T-bar requires NEXT → BKGD; it cannot perform a key-only take.

DSK: independent key transitions and ALL rate

Downstream keys are overlays applied after the scene transition: lower third, ticker and corner bugs in this demo. Program dirty includes them; the clean feed omits them while retaining the scene transition. Off-air keys remain subscribed so they can be brought in.

Click a key's name to fade it on or off at its effective rate, using the selected Fade curve. Shift+click cuts that key regardless of its saved rate. Each key can have its own rate, or inherit the shared ALL rate. Rates and inheritance belong to the running mixer and are shared by every controller; changing a rate does not itself put a key on or off air.

Rate control Action
Click the ALL rate tag Cycle CUT, ¼ second, ½ second and 1 second
Click a key's rate tag Cycle its own CUT, ¼ second, ½ second and 1 second, then return to following ALL
Wheel over a rate tag Step one frame; Shift steps one second. On a key, this sets its own rate
Focus a tag and press F2 Type frames (15 or 15f), seconds:frames (1:15), or 0, c, cut or an empty field for CUT
Type all in a key's rate editor Restore inheritance from ALL
Enter or leave the editor / Escape Save / cancel the edit

Key rates are limited to 0–10 seconds. The ALL tag changes only keys that follow it; explicit key rates remain independent. ALL / K takes all keys off if any is on, otherwise takes all on, each at its own effective rate. This is different from a key-only NEXT take, which uses one common take rate.

For example, set ALL to 12f, the lower third to 30f, and a bug to CUT. The ticker and other following keys use 12 frames, the lower third uses 30, and the bug cuts. Changing ALL to 15f leaves those two explicit overrides intact. Type all on the lower third's tag to make it follow ALL again.

The red tally reflects the keyer's reported on-air state (a click responds immediately, then the mixer confirms it). An amber key number means it is armed for the next take; arming alone does not make it visible.

NEXT: combine background and key takes

The NEXT row in Preview arms BKGD and individual keys. An armed key toggles on the take: off-air keys come in, on-air keys go out. The red dot marks a key currently on air.

Operation Background Armed keys Rate / clock
BKGD on, Cut Cuts to Preview Cut to their opposite state Same take
BKGD on, Auto fade or dip Runs the selected transition Follow its progress and hold/resume Background take's clock
BKGD on, T-bar Follows the lever Follow the manual take; cancel restores their original state Lever progress
BKGD on, media wipe Runs or manually scrubs the ready wipe Switch at the wipe's cut point Wipe clock / position
BKGD off, Cut Unchanged Cut to their opposite state Immediate
BKGD off, Auto Unchanged Fade together Main Fade rate and curve, regardless of individual key rates
Direct key click or ALL / K Unchanged Operates the clicked key or all keys, irrespective of NEXT Each key's own rate or inherited ALL rate

NEXT selection stays armed for subsequent takes. BKGD selection belongs to this page; armed keys are shared by all controllers. With BKGD off the T-bar is disabled. Arm at least one key for a key-only take.

To remove a lower third during a scene change, arm BKGD and key 1, select the next scene in Preview and press Auto. The lower third follows that scene transition even if its independent key button has a different rate. To fade only the lower third and ticker together, turn BKGD off, arm keys 1 and 2, set the main Fade rate and press Auto.

Fade to black

FTB in Program fades the finished keyed program to black at the main Fade rate and curve; press it again to fade back. It flashes while Program is black. The clean feed is unaffected. This is distinct from Dip, which transitions between scenes through a colour.

Viewers, AUX and output wall

For the recorded layout, select Viewers 2, Program preview in the first viewer and Program dirty in the second. Program preview contains PVW, PGM and eight scene slots. Drag the dividers to resize the deck and viewers; double-click a viewer or press Enter while it has focus for full screen. The T-bar orientation, viewer choices and split sizes are local browser preferences.

The slots card controls the multiview last clicked, marked “· slots” when applicable. Click a slot and then a scene to assign it, or use Shift+1–8 for the selected scene. AUX layout selectors and source-page buttons change that bus on the running mixer; [ / ] page a source multiview. These operations are shared, not local viewer preferences. Outputs opens the output wall for monitoring the configured feeds and renditions.

Each browser must support the selected output's codec and profile. The approved October 28 recording used H.264 for both viewers; other setups can offer HEVC or multiple colour renditions. Setup configures the show, sources, scenes, keys and outputs; applying it restarts the mixer. Selecting a viewer does not change the output's encoding.

Performance bar and viewer statistics

Hover or focus a top-bar meter for details; Escape dismisses its tip.

Meter What it shows
LAT Measured cut latency; its tip includes cut p95, deadline misses, AUX drops and input repeat/skip counters
GPU / ENC / DEC GPU work, NVENC and NVDEC utilization separately; tips include device and codec/session details
VRAM / POWER Used/total GPU memory and power draw/limit
WIPES GPU wipe-cache use and available clip details
LOAD / CPU One-minute host load relative to vCPU count, and host CPU utilization; CPU's tip shows mixer cores and sources/core
THREAD Busiest mixer thread, in percent of one CPU core; its tip groups mixer threads and Chromium processes

On multiple GPUs the utilization meters show the busiest device and VRAM the fullest; the tips list devices. A viewer's RTT, FPS and BUF describe its WebRTC connection, decoded frames per second and jitter-buffer residence. Missing or stale player statistics show a dash. Viewer network statistics and the LAT cut measurement describe different parts of delivery.
