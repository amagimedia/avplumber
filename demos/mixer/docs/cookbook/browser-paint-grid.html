AVPlumber · Mixer optimization cookbook

Browser paints numbered on the canvas grid

A browser source's paints are now numbered on the canvas grid: each paint gets the previous tick plus one, instead of its arrival time rounded to the nearest tick. The first paint takes the next tick, so it arrives before the tick it is shown at. DSK keys are drawn by timestamp instead of by arrival, and the compositor reports its repeat and discard counters in its status.

Status: Implemented, measured on the live demo

Why rounding stuttered

Browser sources stuttered while file sources did not. On the 64-source 30 fps show, Chromium painted every window on time: 33 ms apart, at most 7 ms jitter, no drops. The input chain stamped each paint by rounding its arrival time to the nearest canvas tick. For a browser whose paints arrive near halfway between two ticks, the jitter flips that rounding down on one paint and up on the next. That puts two paints on one tick, and the compositor discards one; the next tick gets none, and the compositor repeats the last. Each browser's phase is arbitrary and drifts, so which browsers stutter changes over time, and a scene with many browsers nearly always has one.

DSK keys had the same race one stage later. The keyer drew each key's newest arrived frame when a program frame came in. Key frames arriving near that instant alternately repeated and skipped, which reads as judder on a crawl.

File sources can hit the same flip inside force_fps; see force_fps center_phase.

What changed

  1. Paints are numbered, not rounded. A smooth_timestamps node now follows drm_prime_to_cuda in the mixer's browser chain, which every browser source and key page uses. It gives each paint the previous paint's tick plus one on a 1/fps timeline. It realigns to the arrival time (a resync) in two cases. First, when the arrival-minus-stamp difference, averaged over the paints since the last resync (at most the last 300), passes 20 ms either way; this check waits until 150 paints are in the average. Second, at once when two paints are more than 100 ms apart (discontinuity_threshold 0.1; the node's default is 2 s): without it, the first paint after a page stops painting would be numbered on from before the gap, get a tick already shown, and be discarded. The premultiplied-alpha tag now lives in the mixer's colour node; no filter follows. The compositor's playout maps each stamp to its nearest tick, which for a whole-tick stamp is exactly that tick.
  2. The first paint and every resync take the next tick (round_up). When the numbering is aligned to an arrival time, it rounds up to the next tick instead of to the nearest. With nearest rounding, the aligning paint could sit up to half a tick after its tick; now it arrives no later than its tick, and later paints keep that phase give or take their jitter. round_up is video only; the node refuses it for audio.
  3. Playout counters in the compositor's status. Every 60 output frames, the compositor copies its playout counters; node.object.get <compositor> status returns them under playout: frames, repeats, discarded, overflow and missed_deadlines, plus repeats, discarded and overflow for each input under per_input. They are running totals. A repeat is a tick with no new frame from an input; a discard is a frame never shown, for example the older of two frames on one tick. The control thread reads only the copy, never the playout itself. Trials no longer need debug logging and a restart.
  4. Keys are drawn by timestamp. Each key's frames wait in a queue of at most 6 (kKeyQueue). Each program frame draws, per key, the newest queued frame stamped before its tick plus half a frame: the frame whose nearest tick is the program frame's, the rule the scene playout uses. A key is never waited for: with no frame for this tick, it keeps its previous one. When a seventh frame arrives, the oldest leaves the queue and becomes the key's held frame, so a key on an unrelated clock holds at most seven of its producer's frames: six queued plus the held one. Every key is queued whether it is on or off (Prewarmed DSK keys); only keys above fade level 0 are drawn (DSK fades).
  5. Demo ticker. The demo's crawl page now moves a whole number of pixels per frame (4 at 25/30 fps, 2 at 50/60) instead of landing on fractional positions. Cosmetic, demo page only.

With nearest alignment, a browser could also sit up to half a tick (8.3 ms at 60 fps) behind its tick. The research notes put the worst-case margin of a one-frame buffer below zero and of the two-frame default at about 14 ms. With round_up, the 25 ms buffer keeps the same worst-case margin as the 33 ms default. That margin covers the alignment only: a page slower than the canvas drifts later with either rounding until the averaged difference passes 20 ms, which leaves about 5 ms for jitter at 25 ms and 13 ms at 33 ms (estimate from the thresholds, not measured; the measured browsers ran fast, at 60.02 fps, so the trial did not exercise it). At the start, about 60 repeats still show in the first 90 s while browsers reconnect and prewarm fills.

In the 110-input show at 25 fps (36 browser sources and 4 key pages among its inputs), repeated source frames were 0 in steady state.

At a glance

  • WhatBrowser sources get a smooth_timestamps node that numbers paints on the canvas grid, aligning the first paint and each resync to the next tick. The compositor publishes its playout counters in status. The keyer draws each key's frame stamped for the program tick instead of the newest arrival.
  • WhyRounding each paint's arrival to the nearest tick flipped for browsers painting near halfway between ticks: a discard, then a repeat. On the 64-source show, browser inputs had 1,234 repeats and 1,237 discards in 70–80 s. Keys raced the program frame the same way, which read as judder on a crawl.
  • Latency

    Program latency is unchanged: the default playout buffer stays two output frames, smooth_timestamps passes each paint on as it arrives, and keys are never waited for. DSK key content, though, shows about one playout latency later than before (see Side effects).

    round_up aligns a browser's ticks 0 to 1 tick after its paints' arrivals, where nearest rounding put them within half a tick either way. At the default buffer, a browser picture is therefore on average up to half a tick older when shown than with nearest rounding (estimate from the rule, not measured). In exchange, a paint no longer arrives after its tick right after an alignment, so a shorter canvas.latency_ms keeps its margin: 25 ms at 60 fps gave 8.3 ms lower program latency with 0 repeats.

  • Side effects

    Key frames arrive about one playout latency ahead of the program frame for their tick. Drawing the frame stamped for the tick, not the newest, shows key content about one playout latency later than before (two frames at the default), the timing the same page has as a scene source. Estimate from the code, not measured. Key cuts still start on the next program frame.

    A browser's stamps follow the count, not its arrivals, until the averaged difference passes 20 ms. A page faster than the canvas then resyncs back, which shows as a discard; a slower one resyncs forward, a repeat. The status object gains a playout field; its other fields are unchanged.

  • Cost+1 node per browser source, non-blocking, on the source's pacing loop (Event-loop sharding); no queue beyond its output edge, 0 VRAM: it rewrites a timestamp. Keys: each key, on or off, holds its source's frames stamped ahead of the program, usually the playout latency plus about two frames and at most seven (six queued plus the held one). These are the key browser's ring slots, not copies: a key source also shown in scenes or on a multiview may need a larger browser_ring_size (default 6 at 25/30 fps, 9 otherwise), or its browser drops paints. Status: every 60 frames, 3 counters per input copied under a mutex.
  • RiskLow. A drifting page costs an occasional discard or repeat at a resync: 5 discards in 13,020 frames from browsers at 60.02 fps. A key on an unrelated clock keeps at most 6 queued frames; if its stamps run ahead of the program, it shows the frame the bound pushed out, six behind its newest.
  • Switch it offNo runtime switch. In dmabuf_inputs.py, remove "round_up": True for nearest alignment with numbering kept, or remove the SmoothTimestamps node and return its input edge: the playout then maps each arrival to its nearest tick, the old rounding. The keyer's matching has no switch; revert its change in cuda_rect_overlay.cpp. The status counters are always on.
