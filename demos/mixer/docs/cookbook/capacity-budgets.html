Rate-aware capacity budgets: 110 inputs at 25 and 30 fps

The setup page caps each source type per frame rate. NVDEC now gets a budget of decoded frames per second instead of a fixed stream count, and a fifth browser worker plus pinned raw uploads fill 110 inputs at 25 and 30 fps. Neither 110 ceiling is a validated limit yet.

Status: Experimental, measured at 25 and 30 fps

History of how the per-type budgets were introduced. The current limits for every mode and rate are in Source limits by mode and frame rate.

  • NVDEC counts SDR and HDR decodes together: 1100 decoded frames per second divided by the rate, rounded down, at most 40.
  • Browser counts windows, and the downstream-key pages are windows too. Four keys at 25 or 30 fps leave 40 − 4 = 36 browser sources. The browser service's default capacity rose from 32 to 40 windows, eight per worker, so it starts five workers instead of four.
  • Raw upload units cover raw 4:2:0 files: an NV12 source is 1 unit, a P010 source 2, because its frames are twice the bytes. 30 at 25 fps and 34 at 30 fps; other rates keep 700 frames per second divided by the rate (14 at 50, 11 at 60).
  • Source ceiling is 110 at 25 and 30 fps, otherwise 2500 divided by the rate. Key pages count against it too. At 50 and 60 fps it is lower than the sum of the three budgets (50 against 68, 41 against 61), so not every budget can be filled at once.

The 110 ceiling at 25 fps is older than these changes; before them, the three budgets only added up to 100. The setup page still calls anything above 100 (25 fps) or 83 (30 fps) experimental. When a weighted mix overflows a budget, the excess is shared out among the other enabled types. A request that overflows a budget with no other type enabled is rejected. The v210 4:2:2 sources are outside these three budgets: HDR 4:2:2 has its own cap of 4, SDR 4:2:2 only the source ceiling.

Why a frames-per-second budget

NVDEC load follows decoded frames per second, not the stream count. 40 streams at 25 fps is 1000 decoded frames/s and measured 81 % on the T4. Scaled in proportion, 1100 frames/s is about 90 %: an estimate, not a measurement, and the gap to 100 % leaves room for content that is harder to decode. The 30 fps run checks it: 36 × 30 = 1080 frames/s, where proportional scaling from 81–82 % predicts about 87.5–88.6 %, measured 88.5 %.

The browser and raw budgets at 25 and 30 fps are what fills 110 with NVDEC at its cap: 40 − 4 key pages = 36 browser sources, plus 30 raw sources at 25 fps or 34 at 30. With FFmpeg hwupload, 28 raw sources at 25 fps and 23 at 30 were measured; the rest rely on pinned uploads.

Pinned uploads for every setup-built show

The setup builds each show from demo.example.json, which now sets "raw_upload": "pinned" on the canvas. The recipe's canvas passes into the mixer config unchanged, so every raw 4:2:0 source (NV12 or P010) the setup creates, at any rate, uploads through raw_to_cuda: pinned staging on the source's own CUDA stream, one uploaded frame held ahead of pacing. The v210 4:2:2 sources keep their own v210_to_cuda path. The library default stays "hwupload", so hand-written configs are unchanged. Raw uploads and the CUDA driver lock explains the mechanism.

At a glance

  • What

    Per-rate source budgets in the setup: NVDEC at 1100 decoded frames/s (40/36/22/18 at 25/30/50/60 fps), 40 browser windows at 30 fps and below (32 otherwise), 30 and 34 raw upload units at 25 and 30 fps, and a 110-source ceiling at 25 and 30 fps. The browser service defaults to five workers, and the setup's shows use pinned raw uploads.

  • Why

    The old NVDEC rule was not proportional to decoded frames: it allowed 1200 frames/s at 30 and 60 fps but 1000 at 25 and 50. A frames/s budget aims at the same NVDEC load at every rate; 50 and 60 fps are not measured. The extra browser worker and pinned uploads fill 110 inputs at 25 and 30 fps.

  • Latency

    0 frames added to program and to command-to-screen: a budget only decides how many sources of each type the setup builds, and adds no node, queue or buffer. Pinned uploads hold one uploaded frame per raw source ahead of pacing; that is prefetch, not delay (raw uploads), and there is no latency A/B against hwupload yet. Median cut latency with 110 inputs measured 70–78 ms at 25 fps and 66 ms at 30 fps.

  • Side effects
    • 50 fps allows 22 NVDEC sources and 60 fps 18, instead of 20.
    • 30 fps allows 110 sources (was 83), 40 browser windows (was 32) and 34 raw units (was 23), with 36 NVDEC (was 40).
    • 25 fps allows 40 browser windows (was 32) and 30 raw units (was 28).
    • Every setup-built show uses pinned uploads for its NV12 and P010 sources, at every rate.
  • Cost

    At 110 inputs, VRAM 9.6–10.1 GB (25 fps) and 10.9 GB (30 fps) of 16 GB. Host CPU idle falls from 44 % at 25 fps to 29 % at 30 fps. One more browser worker process. One pinned staging buffer of host RAM per raw source. Code: three small budget functions, repeated in the setup page's JavaScript.

  • Risk

    Not validated: no long soak at either rate. At 30 fps NVDEC sits at 88.5 %, close to the 90 % estimate. With pinned uploads at 30 fps, the upload threads still waited on a lock for 2.2 s per 10 s, so contention remains; there is no hwupload A/B at 110 yet. The rules live in Python and in the page's JavaScript; a change to one needs the other. capacity.md keeps an earlier 42-browser experiment at 110 and its caveats.

  • Switch it off
    • Pick at most 100 sources at 25 fps or 83 at 30 fps, the baselines in capacity.md. The new per-type caps still apply below them.
    • Set canvas.raw_upload to "hwupload" in demo.example.json.
    • Set MIXER_BROWSER_CAPACITY=32 for four workers, and keep browser sources plus key pages at 32 or fewer: the setup still offers 40 at 25 and 30 fps, and the browser service refuses windows beyond its capacity.
    • The budget rules have no runtime switch. To restore the old values in the table above, edit browser_limit(), nvdec_limit(), raw_upload_units() and the source ceiling in setup_runtime.py, and their twins in setup.html.
