Source limits by mode and frame rate

How many unique 1080p sources the setup page allows on the 16 GiB T4 test host (16 vCPU), for every canvas mode and frame rate, and what each source type may use of it. Downstream-key pages count as sources. A request above a limit is scaled down to it, and switching back to a larger mode restores the total. Every limit here comes from the tesla_t4 instance profile; another machine needs its own measured profile.

  • NVDEC: about 1100 decoded frames per second, at most 40 streams. That keeps the T4's decoder near 90%.
  • Browser windows: five workers of eight windows. The count does not scale with the frame rate; only a sixth worker would raise it.
  • Raw upload units: 30 and 34 at 25 and 30 fps with pinned uploads, 1020 frames per second above 30 fps. A P010 frame is twice the bytes of NV12, so it costs two units.
  • SDR v210 4:2:2: no cap of its own, only the total. The page's Balanced mix has none.
  • Modes: an SDR canvas takes SDR 4:2:0 decodes, browsers and NV12 uploads; HDR 4:2:0 adds HLG decodes and P010 uploads; only HDR 4:2:2 takes v210 inputs.

What runs out first

  • SDR at 25 and 30 fps

    NVDEC, at 86–89% with 36 streams at 30 fps. The GPU is near 55% and host CPU 55% idle.

  • SDR at 60 fps

    Host CPU. At 75, 8.5 of 16 vCPUs are busy and the load average reaches 19 (a one-minute reading; it is not a capacity gauge here). A browser window costs about 0.10 core, a raw NV12 upload 0.135, an NVDEC source 0.025. The GPU is at 72%.

  • HDR at 30 fps

    Every SDR input is converted to HLG on the GPU. With all-SDR inputs that work drives NVDEC to saturation above 90, and at 4:2:2 and 110 the backlog ran out of VRAM. The Balanced HLG mix at 90 leaves headroom.

  • HDR at 60 fps

    CUDA compute. HLG 4:2:0 at 61 runs at GPU p95 about 91%; 64 and 66 missed deadlines. HLG 4:2:2 costs more per source: 55 keeps the same margin.

  • NVENC at 60 fps HDR

    The program in SDR and HLG plus the SDR clean feed are three 1080p60 encodes on the T4's single NVENC. At preset p5 it saturated and output fell seconds behind; the demo uses p3, which passes at 70–76%.

How the limit is computed

limit = min(base × share, NVDEC + browser + raw caps), each term a value of the instance profile. The base is 110 at 25 and 30 fps and 75 at 60; 90 at 50 is the 60 fps total scaled by frame rate. The share is 1 for SDR, 0.82 for HLG 4:2:0 and 0.74 for HLG 4:2:2. The caps' sum equals the measured totals at 25, 30 and 60 fps; at 50 fps it bounds the scaled 90 to 82.

Conditions

NVIDIA T4 16 GiB, 16 vCPU, driver R615, Fedora 44 image with CUDA 13.4, Electron 44 browsers. Portrait 1080×1920 canvas from 1920×1080 sources, program with clean feed and two multiviews, NVENC preset p3. Each limit passed the cut-spam gate (cuts, fades and wipes at 4 per second) with 0 missed program deadlines.

An L4 host has its own profiles, nvidia_l4 and the opt-in nvidia_l4_cuarray, which admits 192 sources at 25 fps on an SDR canvas. Their source tables, output ceilings and the runs that failed are in capacity.md · NVIDIA L4; this page is the T4's table only.
