Measuring CPU pressure at 60 Hz

A 68-input show at 60 fps left the host 69 % busy, yet its load average sat near 25 on 16 vCPU. Sampling the ready-thread count at ~20 kHz, PSI and per-thread schedstat show why: about six threads per source wake at 60 Hz and pile into one ~8 ms burst in every 16.7 ms frame, so threads wait for a vCPU while the CPUs idle for the rest of the frame.

Status: Measurement method

Later finding: the burst is a spike about 2 ms wide on the browser windows' own clock, a period of 16,666 us, not something the frame period does in general. That clock drifts 40 us a second against the 1/60 s this page folds on, which smears the spike into the ~8 ms burst described here. The load average is itself built from one point sample about every 5 s (5 × HZ + 1 ticks), so against a frame clock it floats. Browser frame ticks staggered at 60 fps has the measurement, the cause and the fix. The figures below stand as measured on the T4 at the time.

At a glance

  • What

    A way to see the 60 Hz bursts that a CPU percentage hides, from kernel counters and one short trace: the ready-thread count (nr_running in /proc/loadavg) sampled at ~20 kHz and folded on the frame period; PSI cpu “some” from /proc/pressure/cpu; each thread's run-queue wait from /proc/<pid>/task/*/schedstat, summed per process; and perf sched latency for the worst waits. Nothing in the mixer changed for it.

  • Why

    At 69 % busy the host looked fine, yet its load average was ~25 on 16 vCPU. A load average counts runnable threads (on Linux, plus tasks in uninterruptible sleep), and a runnable thread that is not running is a late one. The mixer's work is not spread over the frame: about six threads per source wake at 60 Hz, ~45k avplumber and ~50k Chromium wake-ups a second, and fold into one ~8 ms burst in every 16.7 ms frame. The samples show it: p50 22 and p99 56 ready threads for 16 vCPUs, more than 16 in 60 % of samples and fewer than the vCPUs in the other 40 %, when some of them idle. PSI puts a number on the waiting: at least one thread wanted a CPU and had none 27 % of the time.

  • Latency

    From perf sched over 2 s, the worst run-queue wait was 2.7–2.9 ms for the compositors, event loops, CUDA event thread and encoder, and 3–5 ms for per-source threads, against a 16.7 ms frame: a fraction of a frame, added at the moment a thread is late to it. The runs that counted frames lost none to it: the pinned-upload retest at these 68 inputs ran with 0 missed program deadlines (Raw uploads), as did the after-run (Browser CPU is per painted frame). The compositor counts a missed deadline in its playout and publishes it under playout.missed_deadlines in its status.

  • Side effects

    On the show being measured. The nr_running sampler is one thread in a loop at ~20 kHz and is itself a ready thread, so every sample counts it once. perf sched record traces every context switch while it runs; it ran for 2 s. Reading PSI and schedstat adds nothing the show can see.

  • Cost

    Of the measurement, not of the mixer. PSI and schedstat are counters the kernel keeps anyway; reading them is a file read. No code, node or configuration change.

  • Risk

    What the method cannot say. A ready-thread count says how many threads wait, not which ones or for how long; the schedstat sum says how long in total, not who was late for what; only perf sched names threads, and only for its 2 s. Folding on the frame period assumes the sources keep their phase during the sample. The browser windows do not: at 60 fps they tick on a 16,666 us period and drift 40 us a second against the 16,666.67 us frame, so this fold widened their spike (Browser frame ticks staggered at 60 fps). The per-type costs are averages at one load and one rate, 60 fps. The ~8 ms burst and the 60 % share come from the same samples but are not the same thing: the burst is where the fold peaks; the share counts every sample above 16, inside the burst or not.

  • Switch it off

    Nothing in the mixer: no code, node or configuration change. Stop the sampler; perf ran once for 2 s and left nothing running.

  • How to repeat
    • Ready threads: read /proc/loadavg in a tight loop; its fourth field is running/total, and the first number is nr_running. Timestamp each sample, take percentiles, then fold the timestamps modulo the frame period (16.7 ms at 60 fps) to see where in the frame the load sits. To see the browser windows' ticks at 60 fps, stamp the samples with CLOCK_MONOTONIC and fold on 16,666,000 ns instead.
    • Pressure: /proc/pressure/cpu, line some: avg10 is the share of the last 10 s during which at least one thread waited for a CPU; total is that time in microseconds since boot.
    • Run-queue wait: the second field of /proc/<pid>/task/*/schedstat is nanoseconds spent waiting on a run queue. Take it twice, sum the differences per process and divide by the interval: thread-seconds per second.
    • Worst waits: perf sched record -- sleep 2, then perf sched latency --sort max; the maximum delay column is per thread.
    • Frames: node.object.get <compositor> status returns playout.missed_deadlines, a running total.
