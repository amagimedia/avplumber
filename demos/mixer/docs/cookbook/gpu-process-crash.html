Keeping Vulkan out of the GPU process

A restart that changes the browser page closes and reopens all 29 browser windows. On Electron 41 that crashed Chromium's GPU process: closing windows left a Vulkan cleanup task pending, and a surviving window's shared-texture copy flush tripped a CHECK on it. Vulkan was in that GL-only process at all because, with the GPU blocklist ignored, Chromium runs WebGPU on Vulkan through GL interop. Two Chromium switches, both on by default in the browser sender, address it: WebGPU on the OpenGL ES adapter, so no Vulkan context exists, and no GPU-process crash limit, so a crash never quits the worker; five window-recreating restarts on Electron 44 then ran with 0 crashes.

How the crash was traced

The kernel's trap line gave the offset inside the Electron binary; Electron's published breakpad symbols resolved it to CHECK_EQ(fFinishedProc, &CleanupAfterSkiaFlush) in gpu::AddCleanupTaskForSkiaFlush (skia_utils.cc), reached from SkiaOutputSurfaceImplOnGpu::FlushSurface under CopyOutputRGBAInTexture, the copy behind Electron's useSharedTexture capture. The CHECK fires when a Vulkan cleanup task is pending at flush time. That left the question of why a GL-only GPU process held a Vulkan context at all: the NVIDIA Vulkan ICD was mapped into every GPU process, and the --disable-vulkan the browser sender already passed is not a Chromium switch. The answer is the WebGPU interop: with --ignore-gpu-blocklist, Chromium 144+ enables WebGPU on Vulkan through GL interop and creates a Vulkan context in the GPU process even with Vulkan compositing off.

What changed

  1. WebGPU runs on the OpenGL ES adapter. --use-webgpu-adapter=opengles. On Chromium 152 that turns the WebGPU-on-Vulkan interop and its Vulkan context off, and the NVIDIA Vulkan ICD is no longer mapped into the GPU processes. Closing windows then leaves no Vulkan cleanup task for a later flush to meet. The mixer's pages use no WebGPU.
  2. No GPU-process crash limit. --disable-gpu-process-crash-limit. After three GPU-process crashes, each within five minutes of the last, Chromium quits the worker with “GPU process isn't usable. Goodbye.”; on Electron 41 that took two workers and the show could not start. Now the GPU process is relaunched every time, so a crash never cascades into quitting the worker.
  3. Each switch behind an env flag, on by default. DMA_BROWSER_WEBGPU_OPENGLES and DMA_BROWSER_DISABLE_GPU_CRASH_LIMIT; 0 or false leaves that switch out. Unit tests assert both defaults and both off. +13 lines in the configurator, +12 in its test, +11 in the README.

At a glance

  • What

    Two Chromium command-line switches added by the browser sender's HwAccelConfigurator, each behind an env flag that defaults to on: --use-webgpu-adapter=opengles and --disable-gpu-process-crash-limit. Nothing changed in the mixer's Python or in the avplumber binary.

  • Why

    A restart that changes the browser page recreates all 29 browser windows. On Electron 41 five such restarts produced 6 GPU-process crashes; the third crash within five minutes of the last made Chromium quit the worker, two workers went, and the show failed to start. The upgrade to Electron 44 would not have fixed it on its own: Electron 42–44 do not change the code that crashes.

  • Latency

    0 frames added to program and to command-to-screen: the switches change what the GPU process loads at start, not the frame path; no queue, buffer or node changed. Cut latency was not re-measured. A window-recreating restart ran in 10.5–11 s with the switches; without them, on Electron 41, the five restarts ended with two workers quit and the show unable to start.

  • Side effects

    A page that asked for WebGPU would get the OpenGL ES adapter instead of Vulkan; the mixer's pages ask for none.

    With the crash limit off, a GPU process that keeps crashing is relaunched without limit instead of taking its worker down at a third crash within five minutes of the last: a persistent GPU fault shows as repeated relaunches, not a dead worker.

  • Cost
    • 0 nodes, 0 queues, 0 threads.
    • Memory: the NVIDIA Vulkan ICD is no longer mapped into each GPU process; the saving was not measured.
    • Code: +13 lines in HwAccelConfigurator.ts, +12 in its test, +11 in the README.
  • Risk

    Low. Both are Chromium's own switches. The OpenGL ES adapter is verified to leave the Vulkan context off on Chromium 152 (Electron 44.5.1); it was not tried on older Chromium. Neither switch was measured alone; the measured fix is the pair on Electron 44. The crash-limit switch was never exercised in that run (0 crashes), so what a relaunch does to the windows in that worker is not measured.

  • Switch it off

    DMA_BROWSER_WEBGPU_OPENGLES=0 drops --use-webgpu-adapter=opengles; DMA_BROWSER_DISABLE_GPU_CRASH_LIMIT=0 drops --disable-gpu-process-crash-limit. Set either in the browser service's environment and restart it; the two are independent. No mixer or avplumber change.
