AVPlumber · Mixer optimization cookbook

Ideas we measured and dropped

Every source is a unique, independent 1080p25 input processed every frame; twelve ideas broke that rule, saved almost nothing, or made cuts, pictures or the pipeline slower, so we dropped them.

Status: Rejected

The rule, and where each shortcut would cut it

Why reusing compositor B's picture costs more

The twelve ideas

The cost model said no

  1. 1Subcomposition reuse

    IdeaDraw the Program preview's PVW quadrant from compositor B's picture. Share scaled renditions between compositors.

    DroppedCompositor cost follows drawn output pixels, not layer count. Scenes cover only ~49 % of the portrait canvas on average, so copying B's full quadrant draws more pixels than re-compositing. Best case, reuse saves ~0.01 GFLOP/s of the T4's 8.1 TFLOPS. A whole full-canvas compositor costs ~0.56 GFLOP/s.

    KeptThe PGM tile reuses the finished program. Compositor B idles when no preview is armed.

They would break the rule

  1. 2Share identical media between sources

    IdeaOne decode chain per file, fanned out to every source that plays it.

    DroppedIt would cut NVDEC sessions from 40 to 7 and save 1.5–4 GB. Real inputs are all different, so the saving is fake.

  2. 3720p browser pages or 720p test clips

    DroppedReal pixels stay 1080p. Smaller inputs make every stage look cheaper than a real show.

  3. 4Decode-side resize to 1080×608

    IdeaLet NVDEC write a 1920×1080 clip at the size a full-canvas box shows it on a 1080×1920 canvas.

    DroppedIt keeps fewer pixels than the source. Dropped for the capacity demo; it is not in the code.

  4. 5Keep looped test clips on the GPU

    DroppedReal inputs deliver a new frame every tick. Replaying held frames skips decode work a real show must pay.

  5. 7Pause outputs nobody watches

    IdeaA Janus viewer poller stops the monitor and clean-feed encoders while no one views them.

    DroppedIt saved encoder work only while unwatched, and hid real load. A viewer saw ~1 s of black or frozen picture on resume. A clean-feed consumer that is not a Janus viewer, such as a recorder, would lose picture. More moving parts. Removed from the code.

  6. 10Browser sources at 30 fps in a 60 fps show

    DroppedEvery demo source is a real 60 fps input. A production knob, not a capacity result.

Ruled out for the 60 fps show

  1. 8__GL_YIELD=USLEEP

    DroppedChromium's GPU processes made 0 sched_yield calls in 5 s: no spinning. Their CPU is ~12k futex/s thread hand-offs and ~1.1k ioctl/s.

  2. 9Fewer, bigger browser workers

    DroppedCost is per window per delivered frame, not per process; fewer workers concentrate VizCompositor load (Browser CPU is per painted frame).

  3. 11cpuset split between containers

    DroppedCores pinned to one side idle while the other is short: it idles capacity.

  4. 12FFmpeg hwupload for raw sources at 60 fps

    Dropped69 % of program frames missed their deadline at 68 inputs; NVDEC fell from ~85 to ~26 % because the pipeline stalled (Raw uploads).

The trade was cut latency

  1. 6Narrow prewarm, idle off-screen sources

    DroppedDirect cuts get 80–120 ms slower. Without prewarm, a cut resets the incoming source's playout queue and waits for fresh frames.

At a glance

  • What

    One rule for capacity numbers: every source is a unique, independent 1080p25 input, processed every frame. Ideas 2–5 and 7 break it. Idea 1 saves nothing. Idea 6 costs cut latency. Ideas 8, 9 and 11 gained nothing; 10 breaks the rule; 12 stalled the pipeline.

  • Why

    Capacity numbers must describe real shows. A shortcut that works only because test inputs repeat, loop, shrink or go unwatched makes the numbers wrong.

  • Latency
    • Program latency: unchanged; nothing here shipped.
    • Idea 6 would add 80–120 ms to direct cuts.
    • Idea 7 would add ~1 s of black or frozen picture when a viewer opens a paused output.
    • Kept reuse: the PGM tile runs one aux frame behind the other tiles; the program is not delayed.
  • Side effects

    None on the program path. The honest load stays visible: all sources decode and convert all the time, and every output encodes every frame, watched or not.

  • Cost
    • 0 new nodes, queues or threads; the pause poller's code is gone.
    • GPU: budget for the whole source catalogue, not the scene on air.
    • Forgone savings: NVDEC 40 → 7 and 1.5–4 GB (idea 2); encoder work on unwatched outputs (idea 7).
  • Risk

    Headline capacity is lower than a demo with shortcuts would show. That is the point.

  • Switch it off
    • Nothing to switch off: ideas 1, 4, 5, 7–9 and 11 are not in the code; 10 is a browser source's fps field (config.md · sources), which the demo leaves at the canvas rate; 12 is the library default, off in setup-built shows.
    • Repeated files: a repeat is rejected at load; mark both declarations "independent": true for separate chains.
    • Cut prewarm: the demo runs with --prewarm-cut-scene '*' (every scene). The mixer's own default is off; naming fewer scenes narrows it.
